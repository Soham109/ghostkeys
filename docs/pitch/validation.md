# Validation: student interviews and hands-on test

DoIT's "Badgers Building for Badgers" challenge is judged partly on evidence of validation with fellow students this weekend. This is the script and protocol for getting that evidence, plus a results template. Run both parts with every student you pull in: the interview first, then the hands-on test. Ten minutes total per person is realistic: five for the interview, under five for the test.

Write results down during or immediately after each conversation, not from memory later. Quotes matter more than paraphrases for the write-up.

## Part 1: Interview script (8 questions)

Read the framing line once, then ask the questions in order. Don't lead the answers by describing Ghostkeys before Q5.

**Framing:** "I'm asking a few questions about how you use your laptop day to day. There's no wrong answer, I just want how you actually work."

1. Walk me through a normal hour of using your MacBook. What are your hands doing besides typing?
2. Is there anything you do often enough that you wish it were faster or took fewer steps? (Volume, muting for calls, screenshots, switching apps, anything.)
3. Have you ever set up a custom keyboard shortcut, a Stream Deck, a macro app, or something similar? What happened? Did you keep using it?
4. When you're on a video call, what do you actually touch on your laptop besides the keyboard and trackpad? (Prompt if needed: do you ever tap or rest your hands somewhere out of habit?)
5. *(Now show Ghostkeys or describe it in one sentence: "It turns blank spots on your MacBook, like the palm rests or the speaker grilles, into tappable buttons using the built-in sensors.")* What's your first reaction?
6. What's the first thing you'd want to bind to a tap, if you set this up right now?
7. What would make you *not* trust something like this running in the background all the time?
8. Would you pay for this? If yes, does a one-time $29 purchase feel fair ($19 at launch), or does it feel like it should be free / a subscription / something else?

## Part 2: Hands-on test protocol (about 2 minutes of active use)

Run this after the interview, on a Mac with Ghostkeys already calibrated (don't calibrate live during someone else's test slot; use a pre-calibrated demo unit).

1. **Setup (30s):** Hand them the laptop. Say only: "This palm rest and this speaker grille are both buttons right now. Try tapping them." Don't explain gestures yet. Start a stopwatch.
2. **Unprompted exploration (30-45s):** Watch silently. Note whether they find the right spot without help, how hard they tap, and whether they try anything you didn't mention (double tap, tapping the edge, tilting the lid).
3. **Prompted gestures (30-45s):** Now tell them: "Try double-tapping the same spot," then "try tapping this zone, then that one, quickly." Note success/failure and how many attempts each took.
4. **Debrief (15-20s):** Ask directly: "Did anything register that you didn't mean, or fail to register when you meant it?" Get a yes/no plus specifics, not just a general impression.

Record a pass/fail plus a one-line note for each step, not just a gut feeling at the end.
